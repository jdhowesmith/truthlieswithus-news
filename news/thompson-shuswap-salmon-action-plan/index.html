New salmon action plan targets Shuswap Lake habitat

A regional plan for the Thompson-Shuswap calls for fish-passage, habitat and invasive-plant work around Shuswap Lake and the Adams River. It isn't legally binding.

By Truth Lies With Us Staff

Fri, Nov 20, 2026, 7:39 a.m. B.C. time · 1 min read

XFacebookEmail

The facts at a glance

  • The plan calls for fish-passage work on Syphon Creek culverts.
  • It proposes habitat restoration along Scotch Creek and invasive-plant control at the Sicamous Narrows.
  • It includes sockeye spawning-habitat work on the Adams River.
  • The plan is not legally binding.
The reporting

A new regional action plan for salmon in the Thompson-Shuswap lays out a list of projects around Shuswap Lake, the Salmon Arm Observer reports.

The plan calls for fish-passage work on culverts on Syphon Creek, habitat restoration along Scotch Creek, control of invasive plants at the Sicamous Narrows, and sockeye spawning-habitat work on the Adams River.

The plan is not legally binding, so the projects depend on funding and on agencies and groups choosing to carry them out.

Analysis

Our Take

The Adams River sockeye run is one of the region's best-known natural events, so habitat work here matters. Because the plan isn't binding, what counts is whether funding and agencies follow through.
