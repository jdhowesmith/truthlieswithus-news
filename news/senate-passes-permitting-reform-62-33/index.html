Senate passes sweeping permitting-reform bill, 62-33

The bipartisan bill would speed reviews for energy, mining and infrastructure projects and make large data centres pay for the power infrastructure they need. It now goes to the House.

By Truth Lies With Us Staff

Fri, Nov 20, 2026, 7:32 a.m. B.C. time · 1 min read

XFacebookEmail

The facts at a glance

  • The Senate passed the Bipartisan American Affordability and Jobs Act 62-33.
  • It would speed environmental reviews and permits for energy, mining and infrastructure projects.
  • It would expand federal authority over interstate power lines.
  • Large data centres would have to pay for the power infrastructure they need.
  • The bill now goes to the House.
The reporting

The U.S. Senate has passed a major permitting-reform bill by a vote of 62-33.

The Bipartisan American Affordability and Jobs Act would speed up environmental reviews and permits for energy, mining and infrastructure projects. It would also expand federal authority over interstate power lines, and require large data centres to pay for the power infrastructure they need.

The bill now goes to the House of Representatives.

Analysis

Our Take

A 62-vote Senate majority is rare these days, and Fox News and NBC both reported it the same way. Faster permits for power lines and mines could matter for Canadian energy and mineral exports. Whether the House passes it as written is the next test.
