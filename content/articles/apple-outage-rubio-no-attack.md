Apple Pay, iCloud, Apple Support and other Apple services had problems for several hours on Tuesday, the latest in a string of U.S. tech outages that officials say have no link to each other.

A company spokesperson told CBS News that Apple's services were "green across the board," referring to its system status page, and that the problems were not the result of a cyberattack. Some services, including Apple Business and Apple School Manager, were still having issues at the time, CBS reported.

## The NSA, too

Multiple U.S. officials told CBS News that the National Security Agency's internal network used to store and share classified information also went offline Tuesday.

CNBC counted Tuesday's Apple problems as the fourth major outage in four business days, after Microsoft Outlook on Thursday, the FAA on Friday and JPMorgan Chase on Monday.

## "Not under systemic attack"

Speaking at a news conference in the Philippines on Wednesday morning local time, Secretary of State Marco Rubio said he wanted to be clear that the United States is not under systemic attack.

"We have zero evidence or any reason to believe that any of these isolated or individual instances have anything to do with nefarious actors, with cyber hackers, with criminals, with another nation state," Rubio said, according to CBS News.

Sen. Mark Warner, the Virginia Democrat who is vice chair of the Senate Intelligence Committee, said Tuesday there is no indication the incidents are the result of an attack, CBS reported. He noted that artificial intelligence is creating pressure on companies to deal with vulnerabilities. "AI is making it possible to identify vulnerabilities across enormous numbers of systems at unprecedented speed," he said.
