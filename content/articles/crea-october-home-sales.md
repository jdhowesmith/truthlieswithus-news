Home sales across Canada edged up slightly in October but stayed well below last year's level, according to the Canadian Real Estate Association.

National sales rose 0.3% from September, while they were down 10.6% from October 2025, CBC News reported. The average home price was $656,200, down 3.1% from a year earlier.

## Better than expected

The small monthly gain surprised forecasters. "Not much activity between September and October," CREA senior economist Shaun Cathcart said. "But the remarkable part about that is that it ran counter to … a lot of analysts' — including ours — expectations."

Cathcart had expected sales to slump into year-end after the Bank of Canada raised its benchmark rate by a quarter point to 2.5% last month. That was the bank's first rate change in a year and its first hike since 2023.

## Regional picture

Ontario and British Columbia continue to be the slowest markets, Cathcart said, while the Prairies and East Coast have been "hotter" than many people realize. At the end of October there were 197,000 homes listed on MLS systems nationwide, slightly above the long-term average.

## What's next

Cathcart expects sales to stay fairly flat nationally in the coming months as buyers wait for a clearer view of interest rates. "It could be that we only get another maybe one or two hikes, and that once things sort of settle and we get a little bit more certainty next year, you can see the market come back stronger," he said.

With one hike already and possibly more to come, he added, "I think you're going to see everyone sort of cooling off a little bit more."
