Tiff Macklem will not seek a second term as governor of the Bank of Canada and will step down when his current term ends on June 2, 2027, the central bank announced Monday.

"My time as governor has seen Canada navigate a more fragmented, shock-prone world," Macklem, 65, said in a statement quoted by CBC News. "The Bank has faced these challenges by acting decisively, by using sound judgment in the face of uncertainty, and by serving Canadians with humility and a steadfast commitment to its mandate."

His departure was widely expected. CBC notes the last time a Bank of Canada governor served a second term was in 1980.

## How the next governor is chosen

The bank's 12 independent directors have formed a search committee, assisted by the recruiting firm Egon Zehnder, and hope to finish the process by spring. The board technically makes the selection, but the final decision rests with Prime Minister Mark Carney, himself a former governor of the bank, and Finance Minister François-Philippe Champagne.

According to CBC, the job posting says candidates must be Canadian citizens, hold a graduate degree in economics, speak both official languages at a high level and have experience at the highest levels of central banking, public policy, financial regulation, markets, academia or international institutions.

## A tenure of shocks

Macklem took over on June 3, 2020, during the first wave of COVID-19. After inflation peaked at 8.1% in mid-2022, the bank raised its policy rate from 0.25% to 5% through 10 straight hikes over 16 months, one of the most aggressive tightening cycles in its history.

He has also acknowledged mistakes. In a 2024 speech he said, "With hindsight, we probably could have begun withdrawing stimulus sooner."

His later years have been defined by the Canada–U.S. trade war. Last month, the bank raised its rate a quarter point to 2.5%, its first hike since 2023. On Monday, Statistics Canada reported that consumer prices rose 3.1% in the year to October, above the bank's 2% target.
