Richard Sterban, the deep bass voice of The Oak Ridge Boys and one of the most recognizable sounds in country music, has died at 83.

Sterban died Saturday in Hendersonville, Tenn., from complications of bronchitis and pneumonia after a battle with cancer, his family said. "We appreciate all of your love, support, and prayers," the family said in a statement reported by NBC News.

## From Elvis to the Oaks

Born April 24, 1943, in Camden, New Jersey, Sterban started singing at six as a soprano in Sunday school. By eighth grade his voice had dropped to bass.

He sang with the Stamps Quartet, which Elvis Presley hired to sing behind him, and performed with Presley for about a year and a half. In 1972, he left to join The Oak Ridge Boys, which needed a bass singer, citing his desire to "be in the best vocal group in the world."

## 'Elvira' and a Hall of Fame career

The group first found success after moving from gospel to country. Their 1981 single "Elvira," with Sterban's famous "oom-papa-mow-mow," hit No. 1 on the country chart and No. 5 on the pop chart, sold more than two million copies and won a Grammy.

The Oak Ridge Boys won five Grammy Awards and were inducted into the Gospel Music Hall of Fame in 2000 and the Country Music Hall of Fame in 2015. They performed for four U.S. presidents, from Gerald Ford to George W. Bush.

"That 'oom-pa-pa-mow-mow' on 'Elvira' was pure joy," Academy of Country Music CEO Andy Schuon said. Bandmate William Lee Golden called it a "sad day."

Sterban is survived by his wife, Donna, and five children.
