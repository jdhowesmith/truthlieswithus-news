Peter Storey, the tough-tackling midfielder from Arsenal's 1970-71 Double-winning team, has died at the age of 81, Arsenal announced Tuesday.

His family said he died in hospital after a short illness, surrounded by his family, The Guardian reported.

"Everyone at Arsenal is deeply saddened by the passing of double winner Peter Storey," the club said, according to BBC Sport. "All our thoughts are with his family and loved ones at this incredibly sad time." The club said it will pay its respects at its upcoming home match against Borussia Dortmund.

## Arsenal and England

Surrey-born Storey joined Arsenal as an apprentice in 1961 and made his debut shortly after his 20th birthday in 1965. He made 501 appearances in all competitions for the club through 1977, scoring 17 goals, and won 19 caps for England, BBC Sport reported. He also played at right-back.

He was part of the Arsenal team that won the First Division title and the FA Cup in 1970-71 under manager Bertie Mee. Fans remember him for a stoppage-time penalty against Stoke in the 1971 FA Cup semi-final that forced a replay and kept the Double alive, The Guardian said.

## After football

Storey left for Fulham in 1977 and retired eight months later, then owned a pub in north London. He was later convicted of several criminal offences. BBC Sport said he received a suspended sentence in 1979 for running a brothel, was later jailed for three years over a counterfeiting operation, and served another sentence in 1990 for trying to smuggle pornographic videos into Britain. The Guardian said the counterfeiting case involved financing a plot to counterfeit gold coins.

He published an autobiography, *True Storey: My Life and Crimes as a Football Hatchet Man*, in September 2010.
