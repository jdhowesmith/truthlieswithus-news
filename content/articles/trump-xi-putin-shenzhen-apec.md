Chinese leader Xi Jinping broke with protocol on Wednesday and met U.S. President Donald Trump on the tarmac in Shenzhen, setting the stage for Thursday's first-ever three-way meeting between Trump, Xi and Russian President Vladimir Putin.

Trump arrived for the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation (APEC) summit in the southern Chinese tech hub. According to the BBC, a red carpet was rolled out in front of Air Force One, with a cheering crowd, an honour guard, a military flyover and a presentation of flowers. NBC News reported that a handshake between the two leaders turned into a quick hug.

## A rare welcome

NBC News said it is believed to be the first time Xi, in power for more than a decade, has personally greeted any foreign leader arriving in China. Xi was returning the gesture Trump made in September, when Trump met him at his plane for a White House state visit.

China's state news agency Xinhua quoted Xi as saying "China is a nation that values courtesy and has a tradition of returning goodwill with goodwill." The BBC reported that Xinhua also quoted Xi as wanting a "constructive strategic and stable relationship based on respect, fairness, and equality."

When Trump last visited China in May, he was greeted in Beijing by Vice President Han Zheng, NBC News noted.

## Trump and Putin

Later on Wednesday, Trump and Putin shook hands and exchanged pleasantries before a reception. The BBC said it was their first in-person meeting since August 2025, when Putin travelled to Alaska for Ukraine talks that did not produce a breakthrough.

Putin had landed in Shenzhen several hours before Trump and held about two hours of talks with Xi. Putin was met at the airport by Cai Qi, a close Xi adviser and member of the Politburo Standing Committee, rather than by Xi himself, according to NBC News.

A Kremlin spokesperson indicated short, informal Trump–Putin talks could happen without large delegations, the BBC reported.

## What happens Thursday

The three leaders meet together on Thursday on the sidelines of the summit. No full agenda has been made public. The BBC said the wars in Ukraine and Iran, nuclear proliferation, trade and artificial intelligence could all come up.

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky has raised fears of a plan to end the war being discussed without representatives from Kyiv or Europe. NBC News reported that the talks have also caused anxiety in Iran and in Taiwan.

In a briefing last week, a senior U.S. official told reporters the meeting was unlikely to produce any agreement, calling it an "entry point" and "the first conversation," NBC News reported.

Prime Minister Mark Carney is also at the summit and met Trump on Wednesday. That story is in our Canada section.
