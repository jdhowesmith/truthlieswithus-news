Prime Minister Mark Carney and Chinese President Xi Jinping agreed Tuesday in Shenzhen to lower trade barriers on more goods, a move Canada's pork sector called a big step toward winning back market access lost during years of strained relations.

The meeting took place mid-morning Tuesday in Shenzhen, which was Monday evening in B.C., at a guest house in the city where Xi is hosting the Asia-Pacific Economic Co-operation (APEC) summit.

## What China is giving

China will reduce tariffs on roughly $400 million a year in additional Canadian exports, according to the Prime Minister's Office. CBC reports the deal removes a 25% Chinese tariff on frozen pork cuts and frozen cold-water shrimp. The PMO's list also includes B.C. spot prawns, geoduck and other molluscs, sea cucumber and Greenland halibut.

China also agreed to open its market to Canadian fava beans, chickpeas and lentils, and the two sides said they would work toward a deal on pet food and wild aquatic products by the end of the year.

## What Canada is giving

In return, Ottawa is widening its tariff "remissions," or exemptions, on Chinese steel and aluminum to 32 more items: 23 steel and nine aluminum products for which Canadian supply is either insufficient or unavailable. Those exemptions were set to expire at the end of 2026 and have been extended.

Ottawa says more than 90% of Canada–China trade that had faced tariffs now has reduced tariffs. Canada will also send a "major trade mission to China" in 2027, and both countries declared 2027 a "year of e-commerce."

## Reaction

The Canadian Meat Council welcomed the wider access for pork, though the industry noted that offal remains blocked. Carney told Xi the countries can "work together more broadly, pragmatically to solve problems." Before leaving Canada, Carney said he would raise foreign interference with Xi; Beijing denies such activity.

## Mexico, and what's next

Carney also met Mexican President Claudia Sheinbaum on Tuesday. The two discussed the stalled CUSMA trade talks with the U.S. and said Canada–Mexico trade is up 18% from a year ago. The next major event is Thursday's meeting of Xi, U.S. President Donald Trump and Russia's Vladimir Putin, also in Shenzhen.
