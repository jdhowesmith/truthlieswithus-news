Online banking appeared back to normal on Tuesday after customers at several of the largest U.S. banks reported trouble logging in, checking balances and moving money on Monday.

Customers of JPMorgan Chase, Bank of America, U.S. Bank, Wells Fargo, Capital One and Truist, as well as users of the Zelle payments app, reported problems, according to the outage tracker Downdetector. Chase drew the most complaints, with nearly 4,800 reports by 2 p.m. Eastern, CBS News reported. Downdetector showed service had returned to normal early Tuesday.

## What the banks said

A Chase spokesperson said the bank was dealing with a technical bug, "not a cyber issue." In a social media post, Chase said a software bug in its systems affected other institutions "when we were party to a transaction involving other banks," and that the issue was not systemic.

Bank of America told CBS News its "systems were operating normally" throughout the day. U.S. Bank said its systems were not directly affected but that Zelle payments to and from other banks might be delayed. Capital One said it had not been affected. Zelle said its service was "operating as it should" but that problems at individual banks could delay payments.

## A rough week for technology

The bank problems came after Microsoft restored its Outlook and Teams services on Saturday following a global outage, and days after overloaded routers led the Federal Aviation Administration to order a rare nationwide ground stop on Friday. United Airlines also reported a "widespread system outage" Monday afternoon that it later resolved. Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin said the "homeland is secure," the New York Post reported.

"Each one has an explanation that makes sense on its own, but they all point to a common theme: the complexity and fragility of the systems that we depend on every day," Notre Dame IT expert Mike Chapple told CBS News.

## If it happens again

Security expert Kayne McGladrey told CBS News to "try ATMs, cards, phone banking and branches, which often keep working when the app doesn't," and to screenshot failed transactions and error messages so you can ask the bank to reverse any fees. Experts also warned that scammers often send fake bank alerts during outages, so use only your bank's official app, website or phone number.
