The House of Commons has passed Bill C-41, the government's proposed changes to Canada's medical assistance in dying (MAID) law, at second reading.

The vote on Tuesday afternoon was 225 to 89, according to the House of Commons voting record. The bill now goes to committee for study. LEGISinfo lists it as being at consideration in committee.

The vote came a day after the Liberal government limited debate on the bill. That time-allocation motion passed 163 to 149 on Monday.

## What the bill does

Bill C-41, introduced on Oct. 27, would amend the Criminal Code to allow people to make advance requests to access MAID, The Canadian Press reported. It would also prevent a scheduled expansion of MAID to people whose sole underlying condition is a mental illness.

The bill needs to pass both the House of Commons and the Senate before March 17, the date on which the current law would extend MAID access to people whose only condition is a mental illness.

## The opposition

Conservatives say they support barring MAID for people suffering solely from a mental illness, but have urged the government to split the bill to give MPs more time to study advance requests.

Conservative MP Tamara Jansen said it was "absolutely shameful" that the government shut down debate on a bill that deals with life and death, according to The Canadian Press.
