Fort Myers Beach has a familiar face back in the mayor's chair. Town Council picked Anita Cereceda as mayor and newcomer Tom Brady as vice mayor on Monday, Nov. 16, at a reorganization meeting that began with Cereceda, Brady and returning council member Rebecca Link being sworn in.

Under the town charter, council members choose the mayor and vice mayor from among themselves.

## How the votes went

Both votes were 3–2, according to WINK News and Fort Myers Beach Talk. Vice Mayor Scott Safford nominated outgoing Mayor Dan Allers, but only Allers and Safford voted for him. Cereceda won with her own vote plus those of Brady and Link.

For vice mayor, Allers nominated Safford, who received two votes. Cereceda nominated Brady, who got the three votes needed.

## Who they are

Cereceda served as the town's first mayor after Fort Myers Beach incorporated in 1995. She was term-limited in 2020 and has since served on the town's Local Planning Agency, so that seat now needs to be filled. She received the most votes in the Nov. 3 election, 1,769 according to the Lee County Supervisor of Elections, followed by Brady with 1,511 and Link with 1,335.

Brady is new to council. He previously served as president of Protect Fort Myers Beach, a community group involved in discussions about development and the island's future.

Allers had been mayor since 2022 and had said he wanted to continue. Departing council members John King and John McLean said goodbye before the votes.

## Still to decide

Council still has to choose who represents the town on the Florida League of Cities, the Southwest Florida League of Cities and the regional Metropolitan Planning Organization. King held all three seats. The new council will continue to oversee redevelopment, town finances and the island's recovery from Hurricane Ian.

## Meanwhile in Fort Myers

Across the bridge, Amber Ware was sworn in Monday afternoon as Fort Myers' first Black mayor, WGCU and Gulf Coast News reported. She told Gulf Coast News one of her first initiatives will be "a clear and transparent audit of the city's finances."
