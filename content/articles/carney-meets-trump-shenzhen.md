Prime Minister Mark Carney met U.S. President Donald Trump on Wednesday on the margins of the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation summit in Shenzhen, China.

According to a readout from the Prime Minister's Office, the conversation lasted just under 20 minutes and took place shortly before the leaders' dinner. It focused on talks "underway to reach a mutually beneficial trade agreement" between the two countries. The PMO defined that as a deal that would "create good jobs, boost affordability and fully respect Canada's languages, culture and sovereignty."

"We agreed to continue the work and to stay in close contact," Carney wrote afterward.

## Who was in the room

Foreign Affairs Minister Anita Anand and Carney's chief of staff, Marc-André Blanchard, attended for Canada. Trump was joined by his chief of staff Stephen Miller, U.S. Trade Representative Jamieson Greer, Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick and Secretary of State Marco Rubio, CBC News and The Canadian Press reported.

## "Not the top issue at all"

At a news conference before the meeting, Carney said Canada's growing trade with China is not a top concern for Trump.

"In my conversation with the president, he's tried to understand where we are with China, where we're going with China," Carney said, according to CBC News. "I shouldn't speak for him, but in his hierarchy of issues, it's not the top issue at all."

Carney said Canada mainly sells commodities to China. "We are exporting canola, we are exporting pork. We are exporting halibut," he said, adding: "These are not cutting-edge semiconductors." He told The Canadian Press it has been easier to secure new arrangements with China because Canada's trade relationship with the U.S. is "much more complex."

White House trade adviser Peter Navarro has criticized Ottawa's approach. "Are u really threatening to play China off the U.S.? This is offensive in the extreme," Navarro wrote on X, as quoted by CBC.

## Where the talks stand

Canada-U.S. Trade Minister Dominic LeBlanc has not travelled to Washington since talks restarted earlier this month, but there have been regular phone and video calls with Greer and Lutnick, three senior government officials told CBC News. Chief trade negotiator Janice Charette is usually on those calls. The officials said there is no formal deal on the table yet.

"We have been making progress," Carney said Wednesday, adding that any deal must be "in the interest of Canadians."

## The China deals

On Tuesday, Carney met Chinese President Xi Jinping. CBC reported that China will provide tariff relief on about $400 million in Canadian exports, including frozen pork cuts, fish and seafood. In return, Canada will extend tariff exemptions for certain Chinese steel and aluminum products by two years.
