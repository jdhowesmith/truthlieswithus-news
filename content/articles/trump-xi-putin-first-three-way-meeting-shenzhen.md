U.S. President Donald Trump, Chinese President Xi Jinping and Russian President Vladimir Putin held their first-ever three-way meeting Thursday in Shenzhen, China, on the sidelines of the APEC summit, according to CBC News and The Associated Press. The session lasted a little over an hour at a lakeside villa.

"We are really making history," Trump said. He met Xi and Putin separately beforehand and called both men "friends." He has since left on Air Force One for Washington.

## What the joint statement says

- **Nuclear weapons:** The three "reaffirmed that a nuclear war cannot be won and must never be fought" and pledged a dialogue on reducing nuclear risks and preventing proliferation.
- **International courts:** The statement says the International Criminal Court and similar bodies "must not abuse their jurisdiction" and stresses immunity for state officials.
- **UN and fuel:** It backs UN reform and plans to increase production and supply of refined petroleum products.

## What it leaves out

The statement does not mention Ukraine or Iran. Trump said he raised both in his one-on-one meetings. The leaders took no questions.

## Background

New START, the last U.S.–Russia nuclear arms pact, expired in February, and Trump wants a new deal that includes China. China has more than 600 warheads, compared with more than 5,000 each believed to be held by the United States and Russia, according to the AP.

There was friction with the press: China barred Bloomberg reporter Annmarie Hordern from the three-way session after she asked Trump a question at the earlier Trump–Xi meeting, Fox News reported.

## What's next

Trump and Xi are scheduled to meet again at the G20 summit in Florida in December.
