Iran launched drones at what it said was a U.S. military radar system in Bahrain early Tuesday, its third such attack in five days after a roughly two-month lull in the conflict.

Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) said its target was the radar at the Sheikh Isa base in Bahrain, home of the U.S. Navy's Fifth Fleet, Fox News reported, citing Reuters. Bahraini authorities issued a security alert urging residents to take shelter, then gave the all-clear later in the morning. No deaths or injuries were reported.

## U.S. says nothing got through

U.S. Central Command said the drones were "intercepted without any damage to U.S. equipment or injury to American personnel," and called Iran's claim of a hit "FALSE."

Iran made similar attempts on Friday and Sunday. On Sunday, the IRGC claimed six drones targeted a radar at the Al-Riffa helicopter base. Both earlier attacks were also intercepted.

## Claims of mines in the strait

Separately, the IRGC said two vessels caught fire after hitting mines in the Strait of Hormuz, one reportedly carrying 2 million barrels of crude oil. Those claims have not been confirmed. The U.S. military says the southern shipping lanes are clear of mines, while independent monitors have reported frequent blasts from "unknown projectiles" hitting ships in the area.

## The blockade stays

President Donald Trump has said the U.S. naval blockade of Iran will remain as long as Tehran tries to disrupt traffic through the strait. Reuters has reported that Trump told advisers he may not strike Iran again if there are no further provocations from the IRGC or the Iran-backed Houthis in Yemen.

Oman and Iran have discussed ways to manage shipping through the strait. The U.S. is not part of those talks, and Trump has signalled he would reject any arrangement that gives Tehran control of the waterway.
