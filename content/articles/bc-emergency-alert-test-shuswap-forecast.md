Phones across British Columbia will blare on Wednesday afternoon. The province is running a test of the BC Emergency Alert system at 1:55 p.m. on Wednesday, Nov. 18.

The test alert will go to all compatible cellphones and will interrupt radio and television broadcasts. It will be clearly identified as a test, and no action is needed, the provincial government said.

The message will read roughly: "This is a TEST of the BC Emergency Alert system. This is ONLY a TEST. In an emergency, this message would tell you what to do to stay safe."

## Will your phone get it?

To receive alerts, the province says your phone must be turned on, connected to an LTE network, not set to "do not disturb" or airplane mode, compatible with wireless public alerting, inside the alert area and running up-to-date software.

The test is part of a national test of the National Public Alerting System. B.C. tests its system twice a year. The province issues alerts for hazards such as wildfires, floods, landslides and tsunamis, often at the request of local governments or First Nations. Environment and Climate Change Canada issues alerts for tornadoes and severe thunderstorms, and police issue Amber Alerts. The province is asking people to fill out a short online survey afterward to help measure how far the test reached.
