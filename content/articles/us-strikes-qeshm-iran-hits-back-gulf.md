The United States struck an Iranian Revolutionary Guard missile site on Qeshm Island in the Strait of Hormuz on Tuesday, and on Wednesday Bahrain and Kuwait said they had intercepted weapons launched by Iran.

## The U.S. strike

A U.S. military official said the precision strikes were launched "in self-defense." "The strike was in response to IRGC aggression targeting U.S. personnel and assets," the official said, according to CBS News.

The strike came hours after Iran said it had targeted a U.S. radar installation in Bahrain for the third time in less than a week. All of those attacks were intercepted, U.S. officials said.

Ahmad Nafisi, deputy governor of Iran's Hormozgan province, said the strikes "caused no damage to residential, commercial, or infrastructural areas," according to Iran's official IRNA news agency, as cited by CBS.

## Iran fires at Gulf states

The Iranian army said Wednesday its drones "targeted Patriot air defence systems and drone and missile launch platforms" at Ali Al-Salem Air Base in Kuwait, in a statement carried by state television, CBS reported. Kuwait's military said its air defences were "responding to hostile drone threats."

Kuwait's foreign ministry said "the continuation of these attacks constitutes a dangerous escalation that threatens the region's security and stability and undermines diplomatic efforts aimed at de-escalation."

Bahrain said its air defences intercepted attacks early Wednesday.

## A ship hit in the strait

A ship belonging to the United Arab Emirates' state oil company, ADNOC, was struck in the Strait of Hormuz. ADNOC said one crew member was missing and 11 people were injured, and search and rescue operations were continuing, according to CBS News.

## Yemen

Separately, the Saudi-led coalition fighting Iran-backed Houthi rebels said strikes launched Tuesday destroyed "60 legitimate military targets" in Sanaa and the provinces of Amran, Saada and Hodeida. The Houthis said the strikes killed at least 17 people, CBS reported.
