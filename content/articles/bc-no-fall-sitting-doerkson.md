**Thursday update:** Sixty-four Conservative MLAs, including premier-designate Lorne Doerkson, take the oath of office at the B.C. legislature today, The Canadian Press reports. Doerkson will be sworn in as British Columbia's 38th premier on Friday, along with his cabinet. NDP and Green MLAs are sworn in next week. The NDP now holds 27 of the legislature's 93 seats.

British Columbia's new Conservative government will not recall the legislature this fall, Premier-designate Lorne Doerkson said Monday.

"Our government has an ambitious agenda, and we want to ensure legislation is properly prepared and receives the debate and consideration it deserves," Doerkson said in a written statement on Nov. 16, Black Press reported.

He had previously left the door open to a short session before the end of the year.

## The handover this week

The Conservatives won 64 of the legislature's 93 seats in the Oct. 24 election, ending nine years of NDP government. Conservative MLAs take the oath Thursday evening, as scheduled. The premier and cabinet will be sworn in on Friday, Nov. 20, officially taking control of the government.

"Ministers will get to work from day one, advancing our priorities and preparing a substantive legislative agenda for the spring," Doerkson said.

## What can happen without a sitting

A new government can do a lot just by taking control of ministries and issuing cabinet orders. That could include changes to the Massey Tunnel project, where Doerkson has promised to scrap the planned tunnel and build a bridge instead, and early work on promised urgent care centres and a Surrey women's and children's hospital.

## What has to wait

Some major campaign promises need new laws, so they will wait until the legislature meets in the spring. Those include the promised repeal of the Declaration on the Rights of Indigenous Peoples Act and changes to B.C.'s no-fault car insurance rules.

## A ripple in Ottawa

The B.C. result is also reshaping federal politics. The Canadian Press reports that two of Conservative Leader Pierre Poilievre's senior staff, communications director Micah Green and media relations director Sam Lilly, are leaving for the new B.C. government.
