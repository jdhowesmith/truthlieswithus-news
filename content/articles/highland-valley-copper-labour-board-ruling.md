B.C.'s Labour Relations Board has partially sided with the union at Teck's Highland Valley Copper mine, finding the company broke the Labour Relations Code by having non-union workers and a contractor do the work of striking employees.

Board vice-chair Paul Todd released his decision Friday, Nov. 13, after a four-day hearing between United Steelworkers Local 7619 and Teck, the Salmon Arm Observer reported.

## The findings

The union alleged Teck was using ineligible replacement workers and contractors to do the work of employees on strike. It first claimed seven instances, narrowed to five before the hearing.

Todd found Teck breached Section 68 of the code by having three workers perform work meant to be done by bargaining-unit employees, and by having a contractor do some road work. In all, he found four instances of the employer breaching the code.

## No site-access order

The union sought remedies including a declaration, a cease-and-desist order, damages and an order to have a designated union representative access the worksite.

Todd declined to grant the access order, saying the four breaches "do not justify such an extraordinary order." He said Teck admitted the breaches, which were either in error or limited in time and scope, and noted they were isolated. He said the context did not indicate the employer used the workers with the intention of defeating the strike.

## The strike

The union represents 1,200 employees at the mine, about 17 kilometres west of Logan Lake. The collective agreement expired Sept. 30. Members voted to strike on Oct. 19, and job action began Oct. 24.
