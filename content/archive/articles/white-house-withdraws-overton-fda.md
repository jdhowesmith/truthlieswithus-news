The White House has withdrawn Dr. Heidi Overton's nomination to lead the U.S. Food and Drug Administration, a day before a Senate committee was scheduled to vote on it.

A White House official confirmed the decision Tuesday, NBC News reported. Axios first reported it.

## The committee math

To reach the full Senate, Overton needed 11 votes on the 21-member Health, Education, Labor and Pensions Committee, NBC News said, so she could not afford to lose a single Republican if all Democrats opposed her.

Committee chair Sen. Bill Cassidy, R-La., had earlier questioned whether Overton, a preventive medicine doctor who works as a domestic policy official in the White House, had enough management experience. He had said last week he remained undecided.

## Cassidy's account

On Tuesday night, Cassidy criticized the decision. "Tonight, the White House abandoned a viable confirmation path for Dr. Overton," he wrote on X, as quoted by NBC News. "My conversations with her convinced me she would follow the science and push back against decisions that put Americans' health at risk."

"The last issue was that pro-life Senators sought final assurances from the Administration regarding the Administration's commitment to life issues. The Administration withdrew Dr. Overton rather than make such commitments," Cassidy said.

Before joining the White House, Overton was vice chair and chief policy officer at the conservative America First Policy Institute, where she criticized expanded access to abortion pills in a 2023 policy brief, Fox News reported.

## Who runs the FDA now

The agency has had no permanent commissioner since Dr. Marty Makary resigned in May, NBC News said. Kyle Diamantas has led it in an acting capacity, according to Fox News. NBC News noted the agency is nearing a decision on the abortion pill mifepristone.
