Measles is no longer considered eliminated in the United States, reversing one of the country's biggest public health achievements of this century.

An independent commission of the Pan American Health Organization (PAHO), the regional arm of the World Health Organization, revoked the U.S. elimination status after finding the virus has circulated continuously for more than a year. PAHO announced the decision on Tuesday. The U.S. first earned the status in 2000.

"Losing a measles elimination status has broader implications than losing a public health designation," PAHO director Dr. Jarbas Barbosa said in a briefing, according to NBC News. "It means the virus has circulated for a prolonged period of time, putting families and communities at risk."

## The numbers

More than 5,400 measles cases have been recorded in the U.S. this year, more than double last year's count and the highest since 1991. Ten people have died, according to state health departments, and at least four of them were babies too young to be vaccinated.

Vaccination has slipped. As of the 2025–26 school year, 92% of U.S. kindergarteners had their measles shots, down from 95% in 2019–20. Ninety-five per cent is the level generally needed to stop outbreaks.

The PAHO panel spent four days in Washington last week reviewing genetic data from cases that were not linked to travel, to see whether outbreaks in different states were connected. It also found that measles is no longer eliminated in Mexico or Bolivia.

## What U.S. health officials say

A spokesperson for the Department of Health and Human Services said the CDC has "mounted an aggressive response," including $100 million in additional funding in October and experts sent to help states. CDC Director Dr. Erica Schwartz said in a statement: "Losing a designation doesn't mean we abandon an evidence-based approach. It means we remain focused on stopping outbreaks."

Some health departments in outbreak areas are now recommending an early vaccine dose for infants as young as six months.

## The Canadian connection

Canada lost its own measles-elimination status in November 2025 after an outbreak of more than 5,000 cases. Experts told NBC News it could take years for the U.S. to win the designation back.
