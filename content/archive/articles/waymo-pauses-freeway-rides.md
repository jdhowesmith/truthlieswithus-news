Waymo has stopped putting its driverless vehicles on U.S. freeways after California banned its robotaxis from state highways, saying they pose an "imminent hazard to public safety."

"We've proactively implemented a freeway operating restriction across all of our markets," a Waymo spokesperson said Tuesday, according to NBC News. "Notably, this only extends to our freeway service, and we're working to quickly and safely re-open freeway routing."

## Why California acted

California's Department of Motor Vehicles said it issued the ban Nov. 11 after one of Waymo's autonomous vehicles tried to pass highway patrol officers who were conducting a traffic break on a San Francisco-area freeway, NBC News reported. A traffic break is when officers slow all lanes behind them, often to clear a hazard ahead.

The department also cited a separate incident in which three Waymo vehicles entered an active construction closure on another San Francisco-area highway.

## What continues

Waymo's service on regular city streets is not affected. A Waymo spokesperson told CBC News the freeway construction-zone encounters on U.S. 101 in California did not "involve any injuries or collisions."
