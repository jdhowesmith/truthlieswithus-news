Lee County commissioners begin the hard work today of figuring out what changes after voters approved Amendment 3, which greatly expands Florida's homestead property-tax exemption.

The first Amendment 3 budget workshop starts at 1:30 p.m. Eastern (11:30 a.m. B.C. time) at the Administrative East Building, 2120 Second St., in Fort Myers. It is open to the public, but commissioners will not make final decisions and will not take public comment. Beach Talk Radio says it plans to stream the meeting on its Facebook page and YouTube channel.

## How much money is at stake

Commissioner Kevin Ruane told WINK News the county now estimates it could collect about $126 million less in the first year and roughly $160 million less in the second year. That second-year figure is down from an earlier estimate of about $240 million. "It's still significant," Ruane said.

## What could change

Ruane made a firm promise on the tax rate. "I will not increase the millage rate as long as I sit in this chair," he told WINK.

That means looking hard at spending. Ruane said not all county money comes from the same pot. Libraries, for example, have their own line on tax bills, so money can't simply be moved from the general fund to cover them. "If you want to use libraries and we're open six days a week, well, maybe the cuts require us to only be open five days a week," he said.

He said commissioners will also examine parks, Conservation 20/20, Animal Services, LeeTran and other programs, and that user fees could rise. Public safety, including EMS, fire and law enforcement, will be treated differently. Ruane said he hopes to avoid layoffs, relying instead on not filling some jobs when people retire, and that outsourcing could be considered.

He plans to ask the county attorney during the workshop to spell out which services the county is legally required to fund. He expects monthly workshops until the budget is adopted next September.

## About Amendment 3

Florida voters approved Amendment 3 statewide with 62% of the vote, and Lee County voters backed it roughly 200,000 to 90,000. It raises the homestead exemption from $50,000 to $150,000 in 2027 and to $250,000 in 2028. Homestead applies only to a permanent Florida residence, and state lawmakers still have to set the residency rules. Commissioner-elect Amanda Cochran urged caution in a statement to WINK, saying the county should let the legislative process unfold "before drawing conclusions about the amendment's full impact."
