Nvidia reported record results on Tuesday in its first quarterly report since chief executive Jensen Huang's departure, and said it has stopped shipping its licensed Hopper AI chips to China.

## The results

The chipmaker's fiscal third-quarter revenue rose to $115.8 billion, compared with $57 billion a year earlier, CBS News reported. Profit was $65.3 billion, up 105% from $31.9 billion and above analysts' forecasts.

## China shipments halted

Interim CEO Colette Kress told analysts that sales of licensed Hopper products to China totalled $2.4 billion in the quarter, or about 2% of revenue.

"Our guidance does not have any licensed Hopper data center products included, and we are no longer shipping licensed Hopper data center products to China," Kress said, according to CBS News. The company did not give a reason. The Hopper line includes the H200 chip, which CBS said the U.S. Commerce Department approved for sale in China in January.

CBS noted that Nvidia had earlier agreed to give the U.S. government 15% of revenue from sales of its AI chips in China, as part of an arrangement to ease export controls.

## Leadership

Huang left the company after admitting to an affair with a woman U.S. intelligence suspects of spying for Beijing, a claim China has dismissed as a "smear," the BBC reported. "The board does not intend to reinstate him," Nvidia chair Harvey Jones said, according to the BBC.
