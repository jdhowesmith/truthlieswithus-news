Germany and Britain pushed back on Tuesday against U.S. criticism of Ukraine's long-range strikes inside Russia, exposing a rift over Kyiv's war strategy, CNBC reported.

## The U.S. view

Elbridge Colby, the U.S. Under Secretary of War for Policy, said the deep strikes had not helped end the war or slow Russia's advance, according to CNBC. The comments came at a meeting of the Ukraine Defence Contact Group at NATO headquarters in Brussels.

## Germany and Britain push back

German Defence Minister Boris Pistorius said criticizing strikes designed to destroy strategic military targets in Russia "is quite a stupid thing, I think."

"Instead of criticizing that, we should rather criticize and talk about the terroristic strikes every night and every day on civil energy, water and heating infrastructure in Ukraine," Pistorius said, according to CNBC.

British Defence Secretary Wes Streeting said "it is not for us to tell Ukraine how to fight their war." He added: "There is no doubt in my mind that it has helped to degrade Putin's war machine, buying time and saving lives."

## Why now

The dispute comes as Ukraine steps up attacks on Russian oil, logistics and military targets, including this week's strike that partly collapsed the Kerch Bridge to Crimea, and ahead of Thursday's planned meeting between U.S. President Donald Trump, Russian President Vladimir Putin and Chinese leader Xi Jinping in China.
