Ukraine struck the Kerch Bridge, which links Russia to occupied Crimea, for the third time in four days, according to The Associated Press. The overnight attack used Ukrainian-made Neptune missiles and damaged the rail track, halting trains. Crimea's Moscow-appointed head said rail service would resume around 4 p.m. Moscow time Thursday (6 a.m. B.C. time).

Tuesday's attack collapsed a large section of the road deck and an arch. Ukraine calls it the culmination of a seven-stage operation. Putin opened the 19-kilometre (12-mile) road and rail bridge in 2018, four years after Russia illegally annexed Crimea; it has been a main supply route for Russian forces on the peninsula.

## Putin's response

Speaking in Shenzhen, where he met Donald Trump and Xi Jinping, Putin said: "We will respond to these attacks." He said Russia's response will be "much more painful," and that after the bridge strike Russia won't agree to a ceasefire or to a deal to stop hitting energy sites. He also ordered the bridge closed to military hardware and ammunition.

## Russian strikes on Ukraine

Overnight, Russia fired anti-ship missiles and 98 drones at Ukraine, hitting 18 locations. At least 10 people were wounded across 12 regions, the AP reported.

## Diplomacy

U.S. envoys Jared Kushner and Steve Witkoff are meeting Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy and European leaders in Berlin.
