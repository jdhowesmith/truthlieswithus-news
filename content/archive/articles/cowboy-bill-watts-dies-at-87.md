"Cowboy" Bill Watts, the wrestler and promoter whose Mid-South Wrestling shaped modern pro wrestling television, has died. He was 87.

Wrestling journalist Bill Apter reported the death on Tuesday after receiving a text from Watts' son, Erik. "Dear Bill, my father, Cowboy Bill Watts, passed away today," the text read, according to Sports Illustrated. WWE also marked his death. No cause was given.

## In the ring

Watts began his career in 1962, wrestling across the U.S. for the NWA and in All Japan Pro-Wrestling, POST Wrestling reported. He feuded with Bruno Sammartino for the WWWF Championship before wrestling for the AWA.

## Mid-South

Watts is best known as the promoter of Mid-South Wrestling, which later became the Universal Wrestling Federation (UWF), from 1979 to 1987. During that time he helped create major stars, including the Junkyard Dog, Ted DiBiase and "Dr. Death" Steve Williams, SI reported. He pioneered episodic wrestling television, with storylines that carried week to week. POST Wrestling said the Mid-South TV run is considered one of the best wrestling TV shows of all time.

## WCW and the Hall of Fame

Watts became executive vice-president of WCW in 1992 and was gone by the end of 1993. SI said his old-school approach did not fit WCW's direction on television. He had a brief stint as a booker for the then-WWF in 1995, according to POST Wrestling.

He was inducted into the WWE Hall of Fame in 2009, with Jim Ross doing the honours. Ross got his start as an announcer at Mid-South Wrestling under Watts.
