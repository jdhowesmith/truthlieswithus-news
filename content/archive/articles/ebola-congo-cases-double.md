The Ebola outbreak in the Democratic Republic of the Congo has more than doubled in three months, the head of the World Health Organization said Tuesday.

Opening the third meeting of the WHO Emergency Committee on the epidemic, Director-General Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus said more than 11,000 cases and 5,400 deaths have now been reported, in 67 health zones across eight provinces. The outbreak is caused by Bundibugyo virus, one of the viruses that cause Ebola disease.

"Six months have now passed since this outbreak was declared, and three months since this Committee last met. In that time, the number of cases has more than doubled," Tedros said.

## Children hit hardest

Tedros said children "are paying a terrible price." They account for more than a quarter of confirmed cases and one-third of deaths, and seven in 10 children under five with confirmed Ebola have died.

## Found too late

"Two-thirds of confirmed deaths are still occurring outside of Ebola treatment centres, which tells us that infections are being identified too late," Tedros said. He said contact follow-up remains below target and that insecurity is now one of the biggest obstacles to the response.

## Beyond Congo

"The risk beyond the DRC's borders is not theoretical," Tedros said. Kenya has reported nine cases linked to an imported case, including two deaths, and Nigeria has reported one imported case, according to UN News. He also criticized blanket travel restrictions, which he said hinder the response.

## Signs of progress

Tedros said rapid transmission has been halted in most of the early epicentres. At the start of the outbreak, two laboratories could test for the virus; there are now more than two dozen. Close to 2,000 treatment beds are available and about 3,000 people have recovered.

More than 900 people are enrolled in trials of treatments. A vaccine trial began last month using Ervebo, which is approved for a different Ebola virus, and two candidate vaccines developed specifically for Bundibugyo virus will be added in the coming weeks, he said.
