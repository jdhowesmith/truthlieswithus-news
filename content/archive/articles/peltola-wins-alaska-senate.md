Democrat Mary Peltola has won Alaska's U.S. Senate race, defeating Republican Sen. Dan Sullivan two weeks after Election Day and giving Democrats a 56-44 majority in the Senate next year.

Peltola won. Sullivan, who was seeking a third term, lost.

## No ranked-choice count

Alaska uses ranked-choice voting, but Peltola cleared the 50% threshold on first choices, so no further rounds were needed. Fox News put her share at 50.1%. NBC News projected the result Tuesday night.

"Together, we're gonna put an end to the era of self-serving politicians lining their own pockets at your expense. And together, we are going to put Alaska first," Peltola said, according to Fox News.

The race was one of the most closely watched in the country. Fox News noted Republicans were defending a seat in a state President Donald Trump carried by roughly 13 percentage points in 2024, and counted it as the ninth Republican-held Senate seat Democrats flipped this year.

## The two Sullivans

The ballot listed two candidates named Dan Sullivan: the senator, listed as Dan S. Sullivan, and Dan J. Sullivan, a retired teacher and political newcomer. NBC News reported in July that state and federal prosecutors were investigating his candidacy, which Republicans suspected had been arranged by Democrats to draw votes from the incumbent. Because no ranked-choice round was needed, NBC News said, any confusion over the two names did not affect the result.

## Sullivan's campaign

Before the projection, a Sullivan campaign spokesperson said Monday that Alaskans "deserve a complete and accurate count, and we will let that process run its course until every legal vote is counted and certified," according to NBC News. The campaign said it would not comment on individual ballot updates or outside projections.
