Lee County commissioners announced a hiring freeze starting Dec. 1 as they began what they expect will be a months-long review of county spending to prepare for the impact of Amendment 3, the property-tax measure Florida voters approved this month.

The freeze will place about 80 currently vacant positions on hold, according to Gulf Coast News and WINK News. It will not affect EMS or 911 workers.

"The hiring freeze is going to start as soon as possible. It doesn't sound like it's going to affect public safety or life safety positions, but it is going to be wide-ranging," Commissioner Brian Hamman said, according to Gulf Coast News.

## The size of the gap

Commissioner Cecil Pendergrass said the county expects to lose about $144 million in revenue by the second year. "We're going to reduce services, which is unfortunate," he said, according to Gulf Coast News.

Hamman put it in household terms. "If this was a household earning $100,000, it'd be like getting cut from $100,000 down to $81,000," he said, according to WINK. "You're losing, like, $1,600 a month. So you're doing more than just cutting your Netflix account."

No broad service cuts were approved at Tuesday's workshop.

## Where the money goes

County staff said about 59% of the General Fund goes to constitutional offices and courts, and about 29% supports departments overseen by the Board of County Commissioners, WINK reported. The rest covers shared costs, agreements and state-mandated expenses. Some commissioners want the constitutional officers to look for savings in their own budgets.

"There's 3,000 county employees and 29 different departments that we supervise," Chair David Mulicka said.

## What staff will study

Mulicka said commissioners want staff to separate services into what the county wants to provide, needs to provide and must provide by law. Commissioners also asked staff to examine employee benefits, future raises, early retirement or buyout programs, underused county assets, reserves, transportation subsidies, capital projects, Conservation 20/20 spending and cost-sharing agreements, WINK reported.

County employees received a 5% raise this year, but commissioners cautioned that raises over the next two years are unlikely, Gulf Coast News reported.

## What's next

Commissioners expect monthly workshops as the county moves toward adopting its next budget in September. The process could change depending on how state lawmakers implement Amendment 3.

"Tell us your suggestions. Tell me your priorities. Tell me what you can live without," Mulicka said.

Also on Tuesday, Mulicka was named commission chair and Hamman vice chair, WINK reported.
